# Exploratory findings

Phase 3. Six figures in `reports/figures`, generated by `scripts/make_eda_figures.py`.
Everything here is measured, not estimated.

## F1. Deduplication reshapes the class balance, and the baseline with it

`01_class_distribution.png`

The single most consequential finding so far. Collapsing templated bulk filings changes what
the corpus is made of:

<table>
<tr><th>Corpus</th><th>Documents</th><th>Largest class, raw</th><th>Largest class, deduplicated</th></tr>
<tr><td>Stratified</td><td>19,979 to 17,915</td><td>9.2%</td><td>10.2%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Natural</td><td>20,000 to 8,869</td><td>72.9%</td><td>46.7%</td></tr>
</table>

Credit reporting falls by 26 percentage points in the natural sample. Every other category
rises. The natural corpus loses 55.7 percent of its documents while the stratified corpus loses
only 10.3 percent, because the templated filings are concentrated almost entirely in credit
reporting.

**Interpretation.** The apparent dominance of credit reporting in this database is
substantially an artefact of bulk filing by credit repair firms rather than a straightforward
measure of how many distinct consumers have that problem. It is still the largest category
after deduplication, so the phenomenon is real, but it is roughly half as dominant as the raw
counts suggest.

**Consequence for the modelling.** The majority class baseline on the natural corpus is 46.7
percent, not 72.9 percent. Both numbers are correct for their own corpus, and quoting the wrong
one would flatter or unfairly penalise the clustering result. The deduplicated figure is the
one to beat, because the deduplicated corpus is what gets clustered.

## F2. Narrative length varies systematically by product

`02_narrative_length.png`

Median length runs from 211 words for mortgage complaints to 95 for debt collection, a factor
of 2.2. The overall distribution is right skewed with a median of 143 words and a long tail
past 600.

This is a confound, not a curiosity. Raw CountVectorizer counts scale with document length, and
KMeans under Euclidean distance can therefore separate mortgage from debt collection on length
alone, with no topic understanding involved. Any evaluation score obtained that way would be
real but meaningless.

L2 normalised TF IDF removes the effect. The brief asks for both representations, so phase 5
now has a specific and falsifiable hypothesis: **TF IDF should show a smaller gap between
clusters that differ mainly in length, and raw counts should show cluster assignments that
correlate with document length.** Both are measurable.

## F3. Templated filings are heavy tailed

`03_template_groups.png`

Most documents are unique. A small number of templates account for a large share of the
duplication, on a distribution that is close to linear on log log axes. The largest surviving
group in the stratified corpus stands for 101 filings, and in the natural corpus 347.

This is why a fixed similarity threshold chosen by eye would have been the wrong approach and
why the group sizes are retained as a `template_size` column rather than discarded. How often a
template is filed is real information about consumer behaviour and about the credit repair
industry. It simply must not be allowed to vote repeatedly when centroids are computed.

## F4. Cleaning removes most tokens but few types

`04_vocabulary.png`

Cleaning the stratified corpus takes it from 3,773,821 tokens to 1,741,744, a 54 percent
reduction, while distinct types fall only from 22,576 to 21,033, a 7 percent reduction.

Stopwords are few in number and enormous in frequency, so the Zipf curve drops substantially
without the tail shortening much. The 37.5 percent of types that occur exactly once survive
cleaning almost untouched, which is precisely what the `min_df` gate in phase 5 exists to
remove. The measurement gives a basis for choosing that threshold from data rather than copying
a value from the reference solution.

The top surviving tokens are account, payment, credit, loan, card, bank. These are domain
terms, which confirms that redaction stripping and stopword removal both worked: no runs of X
and no function words appear in the top 20.

One observation for phase 5: "would" survives at rank 7 because it is not in the NLTK stopword
list. Rather than hand adding it, this is exactly the kind of term the `max_df` gate should
catch, and whether it does is a check on that parameter.

## F5. Composition over time, and redaction density

`05_time_and_redaction.png`

The monthly panel shows composition rather than true volume, because sampling is capped per
month by design. Read that way, the mix is stable across the sixteen month window, which
matters: it means the corpus is not dominated by a single episode or a single month's news
event, and a model trained on earlier months is not facing a different world in later ones.

Redaction is pervasive. 81 percent of narratives contain at least one masked run, with a median
of 6 where present, making up 4.25 percent of all characters.

## F6. Complaint concentration by company

`06_top_companies.png`

The credit bureaus dominate the natural sample by a wide margin. This is the same concentration
that produces the class imbalance in F1 and the templated filings in F3. All three findings are
facets of one structural fact about this database: a small number of firms attract a very large
share of consumer complaints, and an industry exists to file those complaints in bulk.

## What this changes about the plan

* Phase 5 gains a testable hypothesis about length and TF IDF rather than a box to tick.
* Phase 6 must report results against the deduplicated baseline of 46.7 percent for the natural
  corpus, and 10.2 percent for the stratified one.
* Phase 7 should check directly whether cluster assignment correlates with document length,
  since F2 says that is the most likely way for a good looking score to be spurious.
* `template_size` is available as a column and should appear in the exploratory reporting, but
  must never be used as a clustering feature.
